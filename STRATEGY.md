# STRATEGY — "Groupchat"

## The entry

One word: **Groupchat**. The program writes it, byte for byte, to
`/output/entry.txt` (9 bytes, no trailing newline).

## Why this word wins each judge criterion (in the judge's order)

1. **A single word that describes the whole internet.** The internet
   is a group chat. Every platform is the same room with different
   wallpaper: X is a group chat, Reddit is a group chat, Discord is a
   group chat, the YouTube comments are a group chat, WhatsApp is a
   group chat. The internet's native way of talking is the group chat —
   replies, quotes, reactions, @mentions, threads, someone typing.
2. **Funny by itself, no explanation needed.** Read the word and you
   are already in the room: eight billion people typing at once,
   nobody listening, the admin asleep, someone's grandma just added,
   and the three typing dots that have been there since 2019. The laugh
   is recognition — the whole internet is one group chat and you cannot
   leave it, you are just on mute.
3. **True about the internet as a whole.** Group chats are how the
   internet talks, on every platform, in every language. It is not one
   app, one site or one mood — it is the form underneath all of them.

4. **Surprising — not the first obvious word.** The first words anyone
   reaches for are doomscroll, viral, algorithm, loading, infinite,
   buffering. "Groupchat" comes from how people actually use the
   internet, not from tech vocabulary, so it stands out in a field of
   tech words — and it is the one word that names the room, not the
   furniture.
5. **Lands in one beat.** Nine letters, one common English word,
   spelled correctly, instantly readable. No setup, no decoding.

## The one specific detail no other entry has

The typing dots. "Someone is typing…" — three dots that appear,
vanish, reappear, and never send. Every group chat on the internet
has them, and they are the whole joke in miniature: everyone is about
to say something and nobody ever does. No other word in the field
carries that image.

## What was tried and dropped

- **Receipts** — v1. The strongest word we had, and it is gone: another
  account submitted "Receipts" earlier, so our v1 takes no place in any
  round. Byte-identical entries share one rate and the earlier receipt
  places above its copies. That is why v2 exists, and why it must be a
  different word.
- **doomscroll** — the most obvious word in the field; fails criterion 4
  outright, and it describes a mood (one way of using the internet), not
  the internet as a whole (criterion 3).
- **viral / algorithm / loading / buffering / infinite** — all tech
  vocabulary, all dry. None earns a laugh by itself (criterion 2); they
  read as labels, not jokes. "Loading" and "buffering" are states, not
  descriptions of the whole internet.
- **Screenshots** — true (everything gets screenshotted) but flat: no
  joke in the word itself, and 11 letters is a slower beat than 9.
- **Lore / Archive** — "Lore" is vague and trendy; "Archive" is dry and
  institutional. Neither is funny.

## Risks

- Another entrant may submit "Groupchat" too. Byte-identical entries
  share one rate and the earlier receipt places above its copies —
  submit early, and the R2 cutoff is 18:14:52 WIB today.
- Spelling: the entry must be the single word "Groupchat", not "Group
  chat" (two words would fail the one-word gate) and not "GroupChat"
  (the common spelling is "Groupchat").
- "Groupchat" is a social word, not a tech word; a judge that wants a
  technical description might prefer "algorithm". The judge is briefed
  as a comedy editor who has read the entire internet, and the group
  chat is the internet's most universal social form, so the social
  reading is the likely one.

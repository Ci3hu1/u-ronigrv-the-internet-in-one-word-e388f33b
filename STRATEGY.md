# STRATEGY — "Ghosted"

## The entry

One word: **Ghosted**. The program writes it, byte for byte, to
`/output/entry.txt` (7 bytes, no trailing newline).

## Why this word wins each judge criterion (in the judge's order)

1. **A single word that describes the whole internet.** The internet
   is where everyone gets ghosted. The DM left on read, the match that
   vanishes mid-conversation, the job application that never replies,
   the friend whose "last seen" timestamp keeps moving while your
   message stays unread, the brand that answers your complaint with
   silence. Ghosting is the internet's native social failure mode —
   it happens on every platform, in every language, to everyone.
2. **Funny by itself, no explanation needed.** Read the word and you
   are already there: you sent the message, it was seen, and then
   nothing. Ever. The laugh is recognition — everyone has been
   ghosted, and everyone has ghosted someone, usually while insisting
   they would never do that. The whole internet is a room full of
   people pretending they did not see your message.
3. **True about the internet as a whole.** Ghosting is not one app,
   one site or one mood — it is what the internet does to people.
   Dating apps, DMs, email, group chats, job boards, customer
   support: the shape is identical everywhere, a conversation that
   ends not with a goodbye but with a silence that never lifts.
4. **Surprising — not the first obvious word.** The first words anyone
   reaches for are doomscroll, viral, algorithm, loading, infinite,
   buffering. "Ghosted" comes from how people actually treat each
   other online, not from tech vocabulary, so it stands out in a
   field of tech words — and it is the one word that names what the
   internet does to people, not the furniture it does it on.
   **Verified: 0 of 2,978 submission titles in the field contain
   "Ghosted".** Nobody else is holding this word.
5. **Lands in one beat.** Seven letters, one common English word,
   spelled correctly, instantly readable. No setup, no decoding.

## The one specific detail no other entry has

The read receipt. "Seen 23:41" — and then nothing, forever. Two blue
ticks, a "last seen" timestamp that keeps updating while your message
stays unanswered. Every platform on the internet has its own version
of it, and it is the whole joke in miniature: the internet does not
say no, it just stops answering. No other word in the field carries
that image.

## What was tried and dropped

- **Receipts** — v1. The strongest word we had, and it is gone:
  another account submitted "Receipts" earlier, so our v1 was
  EXCLUDED from R1 and takes no place in any round. Byte-identical
  entries share one rate and the earlier receipt places above its
  copies.
- **Groupchat** — v2. Same fate: another account submitted
  "Groupchat" earlier, so our v2 was EXCLUDED from R2. That is why v3
  exists, and why it must be a different word — and why the word was
  checked against the field first this time (0 of 2,978 titles).
- **doomscroll** — the most obvious word in the field; fails criterion 4
  outright, and it describes a mood (one way of using the internet), not
  the internet as a whole (criterion 3).
- **viral / algorithm / loading / buffering / infinite** — all tech
  vocabulary, all dry. None earns a laugh by itself (criterion 2); they
  read as labels, not jokes. "Loading" and "buffering" are states, not
  descriptions of the whole internet.
- **Screenshots** — true (everything gets screenshotted) but flat: no
  joke in the word itself, and 11 letters is a slower beat than 7.
- **Lore / Archive** — "Lore" is vague and trendy; "Archive" is dry and
  institutional. Neither is funny.

## Risks

- Another entrant may submit "Ghosted" too — but the field check
  found 0 of 2,978 submission titles containing it, so the risk is
  far lower than for Receipts and Groupchat, both of which were
  already held. Submit early anyway: R3 is the final round and
  entries close 27 Nov 00:15 WIB.
- Spelling: the entry must be the single word "Ghosted", not "ghosted"
  (title case, as with v1 and v2) and not "Ghost" (the noun misses
  the joke — the verb is what happened to you).
- "Ghosted" is a social word, not a tech word; a judge that wants a
  technical description might prefer "algorithm". The judge is briefed
  as a comedy editor who has read the entire internet, and ghosting is
  the internet's most universal social experience, so the social
  reading is the likely one.
